Suan Zen

Suan means garden.

The name came first, and the room grew into it. You park, you walk in past the planting, and the noise of the road stops somewhere behind you. Then there is a counter, a chef, and about two hours.

Nonthaburi, not central Bangkok. That is the point, not the excuse.

The Garden

Arrive slowly.

The address is a soi off Nonthaburi 48, and there is parking — which in this city is a kind of hospitality on its own. The garden does the rest of the work before you sit down.

The Counter

Everything happens in front of you.

No pass, no kitchen door. The fish is cut, the rice is formed, the piece is finished and set down within arm's reach. You can watch all of it, or you can talk through it. Both are fine.

The room

Most of what happens here isn't on a plate.

Birthdays at the counter. Families who take the whole row. The chef leaning over to explain what just arrived. This is the part guests photograph, and the part they come back for.

After Dark

The bar stays open.

Thursday to Saturday the counter turns over and the izakaya opens until midnight. No course, no round — sit down, order, stay.
